What the Touch Can Feel

Three questions about force on the Phantom Omni, answered from the driver headers rather than from the datasheet: what the device senses, what it only pretends to, and how the master ends up pushing back.

Hardware · Phantom Omni / Touch Stack · OpenHaptics HDAPI + ROS 2 Humble Project · teleop_left_master_right_slave
Question one

Can we read the torque value?

PARTLY

You can read a number called torque. It is the torque the driver is commanding, not the torque the arm is experiencing. The distinction changes what you are allowed to build on top of it.

There is no torque sensor on this hardware. The Phantom Omni has optical encoders on its joints and nothing else. It measures position. It does not measure force or torque anywhere, including at the stylus.

The giveaway is in /usr/include/HD/hdDefines.h: HD_CURRENT_JOINT_TORQUE is listed under both the get parameters and the set parameters. It is the command channel to the three base motors. Reading it back tells you what you asked the motors to do.

↕ Attribute Nature
↓ HD_CURRENT_JOINT_TORQUE Torque commanded to the 3 base motors. Useful as a command log. Commanded
↓ HD_CURRENT_GIMBAL_TORQUE Reads zero here — the Omni gimbal is passive, not actuated. Commanded
↓ HD_CURRENT_FORCE The Cartesian force command, after clamping. Same information. Commanded
↑ HD_CURRENT_JOINT_ANGLES Genuinely sensed, straight from the encoders. Measured
↑ HD_MOTOR_TEMPERATURE Thermal estimate, 0–1 normalised. Worth watching on stiff runs. See caveat below. Probably modelled

The one signal that responds to the outside world

Tracking error. The slave is commanded to a target through a spring of known gain, so if it cannot arrive, something is resisting it: 

estimated_load ≈ stiffness_n_per_m × |target − actual|

monitor_torque.sh prints this as est_load. It is an inference from a commanded spring, not a reading, and it carries real limits: it cannot separate your hand from friction, gravity, or the arm reaching a workspace limit; it saturates once error passes max_position_error_m (0.04 m) or force hits max_force_n; and the Omni's cable-drive stiction sets a noise floor of a few tenths of a newton.

Question two

Can we read it directly from the robot, not calculated?

NO

The torque value never comes back from the robot. There is no channel for it to come back on.

Sorting every attribute the driver defines by direction of travel makes the interface obvious. Position goes up the wire, torque goes down it. 

device → PC   encoder counts · button bits · safety and inkwell switches
              (plus a pinch analog value on styli that have one)

PC → device   motor DAC values for the 3 actuated base joints

That is the whole interface. When you call hdGetDoublev(HD_CURRENT_JOINT_TORQUE, …) you are not querying the robot at all — you are reading a variable in your own PC's memory that your own code wrote a moment earlier, on its way to the DAC. The USB packet coming back from the device has no torque field in it.

I also grepped the OpenHaptics headers for any current-sense, thermistor, strain-gauge or load-cell symbol. Nothing. The sensing hardware is not there to expose.

Caveat on motor temperature

HD_MOTOR_TEMPERATURE is listed above as measured, and that is probably wrong. There is no temperature channel in the upstream data, and the driver has everything it needs to compute an I²t estimate from commanded current — so it is most likely modelled. The headers cannot settle it.

A clean test if it matters: from cold, with zero force commanded, back-drive the arm hard by hand for a minute. A real sensor registers the warming. A model shows nothing, because no current was commanded.

If you need true torque

Nothing software-only qualifies — everything below the hardware line is calculated by definition.

  • External F/T sensor at the tip. An ATI Nano17 or Nano25 between stylus and tool, on its own DAQ, published as a separate topic. The only genuine reading, and it measures contact at the tool rather than motor effort — usually the thing you actually care about.
  • A different arm. No PHANToM/Touch variant has F/T sensing, so upgrading within the family will not help. Franka Emika has real joint torque sensors on all seven axes.
  • Disturbance observer. Model the arm's inertia, friction and gravity, predict the motion the commanded torque should produce, attribute the discrepancy to external load. Better than raw position error, still an estimate.
Question three

If the slave meets a constraint, can the master feel it?

YES

This is bilateral teleoperation, and it now ships in the bridge behind FORCE_REFLECT=true. The master runs the touch_slave node rather than touch_publisher specifically so its force path is live and ready to be fed.

What you feel is the slave's failure to track, not a sensed contact force — the same inference as question one, rendered under your hand.

Mechanism

The trigger

The hard part is not producing force — it is deciding when. A slave always lags its target a little while accelerating, so naive reflection makes empty space feel syrupy. The threshold is therefore not a constant.

  1. Slave is commanded to a target

    The bridge maps your master delta through the axis map and publishes it at 100 Hz.

  2. Error is measured against that target

    Preferably from /touch_right/tracking_error, computed inside the 200 Hz servo loop against the exact target the servo saw. Falls back to differencing our target with the reported pose.

  3. The threshold adapts to commanded speed

    threshold = deadband + velocity_comp × target_speed — free-motion lag scales with speed, so the bar rises as you move faster. This is what keeps empty space free.

  4. Excess error engages reflection

    Beyond the threshold, force ramps as gain × excess, capped at reflect_max_n.

  5. Direction is mapped back and negated

    The error points where the slave still needs to go — into the obstruction. Inverting the axis map and negating puts the push against the hand that is driving into the constraint.

  6. Hysteresis releases lower than it engages

    Release at threshold × 0.6, so error hovering at the boundary cannot chatter the force on and off.

Two details that are easy to miss. Force is republished every tick, because the master node zeroes force on commands older than stale_timeout_s (0.05 s) — publishing only on change makes it stutter, and the bridge refuses to run reflection below 40 Hz for that reason. And engaging the clutch decays the force to zero: your hand is off task, and a live push would fight the repositioning.

What it feels like

Blocking the slave with your hand is unmistakable, and the direction is correct. But both devices max at roughly 0.88 N continuous, so expect "something is stopping it" rather than a convincing hard wall. And because the trigger is a tracking-error threshold, a workspace limit, heavy friction and a real obstacle all feel identical.

Tuning

Gains, and the loop that wants to oscillate

Master pushes slave, slave error pushes master, and your hand closes the loop. Stiff on both ends oscillates — the classic bilateral failure — and the round-trip delay of two ROS hops plus two servo loops eats the phase margin that would otherwise save you.

KnobDefaultWhat it does
REFLECT_GAIN9.0 N/mForce per metre of excess error. Roughly 0.25× the slave spring — not equal to it.
REFLECT_DEADBAND_M0.004 mFixed part of the threshold. Raise if free space feels draggy.
REFLECT_VELOCITY_COMP0.05 sThreshold growth per unit of commanded speed.
REFLECT_RELEASE_RATIO0.6Hysteresis. Lower means more reluctant to let go.
REFLECT_MAX_N0.8 NClamp before the master's own max_force_n.
REFLECT_ALPHA0.2Low-pass. Lower is smoother and more stable.

The symptom to watch for is a buzz or growl that builds while you hold both arms still in contact. That is the loop going unstable, and the fix is always to lower REFLECT_GAIN first, then REFLECT_ALPHA. Soft master with stiff slave is stable; stiff both ways growls. 

# Terminal 3, with reflection on
FORCE_REFLECT=true REFLECT_GAIN=9.0 REFLECT_MAX_N=0.8 \
SCALE=1.0 AXIS_MAP=x,y,z REPORT=true \
$HOME/HIL-RL/projects/teleop_left_master_right_slave/scripts/run_bridge.sh

With REPORT=true each line ends in the reflection state, so you can watch the trigger rather than guess at it: 

| reflect=ENGAGED threshold=0.0140m excess=0.0110m force=0.099N
