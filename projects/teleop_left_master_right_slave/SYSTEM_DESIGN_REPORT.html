Bilateral Teleoperation Report

Two Phantom Omni haptic devices coupled as master and slave: how position travels one way, how force comes back, the three control loops involved, and what each design decision was measured against.

Hardware · 2 × Phantom Omni / Touch Stack · OpenHaptics HDAPI + ROS 2 Humble Repo · HIL-RL/projects/teleop_left_master_right_slave
1

What the system does

The left Omni is held by the operator. The right Omni mirrors its motion. When the right arm meets something it cannot push through, the left arm pushes back against the operator's hand. That return path is what makes it bilateral rather than a one-way remote control.

The whole thing is built on one asymmetry worth stating at the top, because every design decision downstream follows from it: the hardware measures position and only position. Each Omni has optical encoders on its joints and no force or torque sensor anywhere. Every newton in this report is a value computed on the PC and sent outward. Nothing measures what the arms actually feel.

So "the master feels the obstacle" is shorthand. What the master actually renders is an inference: the slave was commanded somewhere, encoders say it did not arrive, therefore something is in the way.

Three loops, three rates

Rate discipline is where most of the subtle behaviour lives. There are three software rates plus one mechanical loop, and they are not the same number.

LoopRateRuns inJob
HDAPI servo~1 kHzhdCallback, C++Read encoders, compute spring force, write motors.
ROS publish200 Hznode timer, C++Publish pose, error, telemetry.
Bridge100 HzPythonMap master to target, decide reflection force.
Operator~10 HzphysicalUnmodelled. This is the loop that oscillates.

The staleness rule. The device node zeroes force on any command older than stale_timeout_s = 50 ms. Both the target and the reflected force must therefore be republished every tick, not only when they change. A bridge publishing on change alone makes the slave stutter and the reflected force cut in and out — and the bridge refuses to run reflection below 40 Hz for exactly this reason.

4

Forward path: master position to slave target

Eight stages, in order. Each exists to solve a specific failure observed on the hardware.

Anchor-relative versus absolute

By default the mapping is relative: the slave reproduces the master's displacement from an anchor captured at startup, not its absolute coordinate. This is what makes clutching possible — hold button 2, the slave freezes, reposition your hand, release, and both anchors are retaken so nothing jumps.

The cost is a permanent offset. Measured on this rig, with both arms parked:

AxisMasterSlaveOffset
x−20.1 mm+14.3 mm+34.5 mm
y−111.3 mm−121.2 mm−9.9 mm
z−20.8 mm−29.6 mm−8.8 mm

At that instant the slave's tracking error was 1.1 mm — it was going exactly where commanded. The offset is the anchor difference, not a control failure. ABSOLUTE_AXES=y makes Y command the master's absolute coordinate instead, driving the height gap to zero at the cost of clutching on that axis.

5

The slave controller

A PD law in Cartesian space, evaluated in the ~1 kHz servo callback. Position error drives a spring; measured velocity provides damping. 

err   = clamp(target − position, ±max_position_error_m)   // ±0.04 m
force = stiffness · err − damping · velocity              // 35 N/m, 0.20 N·s/m
force = force + force_cmd                                 // gravity comp bias
force = clampNorm(force, max_force_n)                     // 1.5 N
hdSetDoublev(HD_CURRENT_FORCE, force)

Three properties follow directly from this and explain most observed behaviour:

  • Authority is bounded twice. Error is clamped at 40 mm before the spring sees it, so maximum spring output is 35 × 0.04 = 1.4 N regardless of how far the target is. Then the total is clamped at 1.5 N.
  • Holding position costs force. The Omni is not gravity compensated, so on the vertical axis the spring spends itself lifting the arm before it can track. This is why the vertical axis runs short first, and why the force_cmd bias input exists.
  • Tracking error is the only outward-facing signal. Position comes from encoders, so error responds when the world resists. Everything else in the force path is a value we chose.
6

Return path: the reflection controller

This is the part that took the most iteration, and the current design is the third attempt. The naive version — force proportional to tracking error — does not work, because free motion always carries error. The servo lags whenever the target moves, so any error threshold either fires during free motion or misses light contact.

The trigger: stall, not magnitude

An obstacle is not "large error". It is commanded to move and not moving. Free motion has the slave travelling; an obstruction has it stopped. Two independent conditions must both hold.

The threshold is speed-dependent because free-motion lag scales with speed: threshold = deadband + velocity_comp × commanded_speed. The bar rises as you move faster, so a fast sweep through empty space stays free while an obstruction at the same speed still trips it.

Direction

The error vector points where the slave still needs to go — into the obstruction. To resist the hand driving it there, that vector is negated and passed through the inverse axis map back into master axes. Since the forward map is a signed permutation, its inverse is master[src] = sign × slave[i]. Verified to oppose hand motion for identity and non-trivial maps alike, which is the case that would otherwise fail silently.

7

Stability, and one fix that failed

The outermost loop runs through the operator: rendered force moves the hand, the hand moves the master, the master moves the target, the target changes the error, the error changes the force. Negative feedback with roughly 20–30 ms of round trip. It rings.

Three measures are in place, in descending order of confidence:

  1. Bounded gain. Reflection gain is kept well under the slave's spring constant. 12 N/m against 35 N/m.
  2. Slew limit. 8 N/s caps how fast force can change, bounding loop gain at high frequency without softening the steady-state wall the way a lower gain would.
  3. Filtered derivatives. Commanded speed and slave speed are both low-passed. Unfiltered, they injected encoder quantisation straight into the threshold and therefore into the force, scaling with gain.

Master-velocity damping is implemented but disabled, deliberately. Damping is the textbook cure for a ringing teleoperation loop, and it made things dramatically worse here. The velocity estimate is a low-passed derivative whose force arrives after another round trip — late enough that it lands in phase with the motion and pumps it. A closed-loop simulation of the shipped code showed peak-to-peak force rising from 0.007 N to 0.647 N as damping went 0 → 4 N·s/m, with hand excursion growing from 0.28 mm to 17.4 mm. The knob remains, defaulted to zero, because the simulation's hand model is a guess.

The symptom to watch for is a buzz that builds while both arms are held still in contact. That is the loop diverging, and the first response is to lower the gain, then the filter constant.

What is measured, what is inferred

Worth a section of its own, because the telemetry invites over-trust. In the entire servo loop there are three reads from the hardware: 

hdGetDoublev(HD_CURRENT_TRANSFORM, transform);    // position, from encoders
hdGetDoublev(HD_CURRENT_VELOCITY, velocity);      // encoder derivative
hdGetIntegerv(HD_CURRENT_BUTTONS, &buttons);      // switch bits
QuantityNatureNotes
pose, joint_anglesmeasuredEncoders. The only physical input.
tracking_errorpart measuredMeasured position minus commanded target. The only signal that reacts to the world.
applied_forcecommandedWhat we computed and set, copied out en route to the motors.
joint_torquecommandedSame command in joint space. Listed by OpenHaptics as both a get and a set parameter.
last_forcecommandedPost-driver-pipeline. Its gap from applied_force reveals the driver rewriting us.
est_loadinferredstiffness × tracking_error. Cannot separate contact from friction, gravity, or a workspace limit.

Four OpenHaptics capabilities can modify a force command after hdSetDoublev and before the motors: HD_MAX_FORCE_CLAMPING, HD_FORCE_RAMPING, HD_SOFTWARE_FORCE_LIMIT, HD_ONE_FRAME_LIMIT. The node now reports their state at startup and publishes last_force so the difference is visible rather than assumed.

How force and torque are obtained through HDAPI

Everything the system knows about force passes through a handful of HDAPI calls, so it is worth walking the actual frame rather than describing it abstractly.

The frame contract

HDAPI is frame-based. State reads and force writes are only valid between hdBeginFrame and hdEndFrame, and the pair must run inside a scheduler callback registered with hdScheduleAsynchronous at the device servo rate. Reading device state outside a frame returns stale or invalid data, which is why the node's capability report is emitted from inside the first servo callback rather than from the constructor. 

// registered once, then invoked by the driver at ~1 kHz
hdBeginFrame(hHD);

  hdGetDoublev(HD_CURRENT_TRANSFORM, transform);   // 4x4, translation in [12..14]
  hdGetDoublev(HD_CURRENT_VELOCITY, velocity);     // mm/s
  hdGetIntegerv(HD_CURRENT_BUTTONS, &buttons);

  // ... compute force on the PC: spring, damper, external command ...

  hdSetDoublev(HD_CURRENT_FORCE, force);           // N, device axes

  hdGetDoublev(HD_LAST_FORCE, last_force);         // previous frame, post-pipeline
  hdGetDoublev(HD_CURRENT_JOINT_TORQUE, jt);       // same command, joint space
  hdGetDoublev(HD_MOTOR_TEMPERATURE, temp);

hdEndFrame(hHD);                                   // driver pipeline runs here

Units and frames

  • Position arrives in millimetres inside a column-major 4×4 transform. The node multiplies by pos_scale = 0.001 to get metres, then applies optional per-axis inversion and an X/Y swap so both devices report in a consistent ROS frame.
  • Force is set in newtons in the same device axes. The node therefore has to map its ROS-frame force back into device axes before setting it — the inverse of the position mapping, applied in mapRosForceToDevice. Getting this backwards would render force in the wrong direction while every ROS topic still looked correct.
  • Torque is in newton-metres on the three base joints.

Cartesian force versus joint torque

HD_CURRENT_FORCE and HD_CURRENT_JOINT_TORQUE are two views of one command, related by the manipulator Jacobian the driver holds internally: 

tau = J(q)^T · F

Setting a Cartesian force lets the driver do that projection. Reading joint torque back shows the result per motor. Nothing new enters between the two — it is a change of coordinates on a value that originated on the PC. On a device with a passive gimbal, as here, HD_CURRENT_GIMBAL_TORQUE reads zero because those joints have no actuators.

Why none of it is a measurement

The decisive evidence is structural rather than empirical. HD_CURRENT_FORCE, HD_CURRENT_TORQUE, HD_CURRENT_JOINT_TORQUE and HD_CURRENT_GIMBAL_TORQUE all appear in both the get-parameter and set-parameter lists in hdDefines.h. They are command channels. A get on a command channel returns what was last written to it.

Sorting the whole attribute set by direction of travel makes the interface plain: the upstream packet carries encoder counts, button bits, safety and inkwell switch states, and on equipped styli a pinch value. The downstream packet carries motor DAC values for three actuated joints. There is no force field in either direction, and a search of the headers finds no current-sense, thermistor, strain-gauge or load-cell symbol to expose one.

What HD_LAST_FORCE adds

This is the one readback that carries information we did not already have. hdEndFrame does not simply forward the value that was set; the four enable/disable capabilities act on it first. So: 

HD_CURRENT_FORCE  (read in-frame)  ==  what we set, our own variable
HD_LAST_FORCE     (previous frame) ==  what the driver actually shipped

difference                        ==  the driver's own clamping / ramping

Publishing both is what makes the drift column in Terminal 4 possible, and it is the only way to detect a silent ceiling: a MAX_FORCE_N above the device nominal maximum would otherwise be quietly reduced with no indication anywhere in the ROS graph.

Deriving load from position instead

Since force cannot be measured, the only route to an external-load figure is inference from motion. The general form is a disturbance observer: 

tau_external = tau_commanded − ( M(q)q̈ + C(q,q̇)q̇ + G(q) + F_friction )

What this system actually computes is the crudest member of that family, with inertia, coriolis, gravity and friction all set to zero, valid only at rest: 

est_load = stiffness_n_per_m × |tracking_error|

Its error floor is set by the Omni's cable-drive stiction — a few tenths of a newton, against a usable range of 0.88 N continuous. Improving it means modelling friction and inertia, and double-differentiating encoder position for q̈ is noisy enough that the filtering required reintroduces the same lag that destabilised the damping term in section 7. That trade is why the estimate was left crude rather than half-improved.

Terminals 4 and 5: building a comparison

The two monitor terminals exist because the control terminals cannot answer the questions that came up during tuning. Terminal 3 prints what the bridge asked for; that is the wrong side of two clamps. Diagnosing "is the master doing what I think" needs the value that reached the motors, on both arms at once, on one line.

Terminal 4 — monitor_forces.sh

A read-only node subscribing to five topics and printing them as one aligned row per sample. 

       fx     fy     fz     |F|             fx     fy     fz     |F|      track_err  est_load
LEFT  +0.000 +0.000 +0.000 | 0.000 N  RIGHT +0.550 +0.000 +0.137 | 0.567 N   0.0008 m   0.028 N
LEFT  -0.620 +0.000 -0.155 | 0.639 N  RIGHT +0.900 +0.000 +0.225 | 0.928 N   0.0210 m   0.735 N  <-- resisted

Design decisions behind it, each answering a question that arose in practice:

ChoiceReason
reads applied_force, not the bridge's own value It is captured after both clamps, so it shows what the motors were told rather than what was requested.
both arms on one line The interesting fact is a relationship: LEFT should be zero while RIGHT drives, and go negative when RIGHT stalls. Two scrolling windows cannot show that.
sign is preserved, not just magnitude The sign is the correctness check on the inverse axis map. A magnitude-only display would hide a reversed reflection entirely.
track_err and est_load alongside Places the one physically-responsive signal next to the two commanded ones, so over-trust is harder.
drift column from last_force Surfaces the driver rewriting our command. Added once the four HDAPI capabilities were discovered.
staleness marker after 0.5 s A frozen number and a dead node look identical otherwise. This was the fastest way to catch a node that quietly exited.
periodic header reprint Column meaning stays available while scrolling, without a cursor-control TUI that would corrupt a piped log.

Terminal 5 — monitor_torque.sh

Single-device depth rather than two-device comparison. Three modes: summary aggregates joint torque, gimbal torque, applied force, tracking error and motor temperature into one line; raw echoes the joint-torque topic directly; and temperature watches the thermal estimate, which matters when running above the continuous force rating. It also refuses to start with a clear message when the device was launched without PUBLISH_TELEMETRY=true, since the topics simply do not exist in that case.

Its header states, unprompted, that every torque value shown is commanded. That warning is in the script because the display is otherwise indistinguishable from a sensor readout, and the number is easy to cite as one.

check_axes.sh — separating two lookalike faults

Added when the slave stopped following on one axis. From the operator's chair a mapping error and a range limitation are indistinguishable, and they have opposite fixes. The tool compares commanded travel against achieved travel per axis and classifies:

follow ratioVerdictFix
> 0.7trackingnone
0.2 – 0.7PARTIAL, cannot reach targetauthority: stiffness, force headroom, gravity comp
−0.2 – 0.2STUCK, commanded but not movingauthority, or a workspace limit
< −0.2INVERTEDflip that axis in AXIS_MAP

The classification is a pure function of commanded and actual travel, kept separate from the ROS plumbing so it can be exercised without hardware. The bridge carries a complementary readout — a reach mm summary marking an axis @LIMIT when the bridge's own clamp is the ceiling and SHORT when the slave simply could not get there. Between them, a ceiling imposed by configuration is distinguishable from a physical one.

All three monitors are strictly read-only: they subscribe and print, and publish nothing. They can be started and stopped freely during a session without disturbing the control loops or re-acquiring a device.

Verification status

Being explicit about this, since much of the above was validated in simulation rather than on the arms.

Verified on hardware

  • Both devices publishing at expected rates; 1501 pose samples over 3 s from each.
  • Anchor offsets measured; slave tracking error at rest 1.1 mm on Y.
  • Driver builds clean; telemetry topics live.

Verified in test harness against shipped source

  • Axis map parsing, rejection of malformed input, forward/inverse round trip.
  • Reflection direction opposes hand motion under identity and permuted maps.
  • Stall gate: zero force in all free-motion cases, unchanged in contact.
  • Hysteresis engages and releases once, no chatter at the boundary.
  • Clutch re-anchoring produces exactly zero delta, so no jump on release.
  • Per-axis versus norm clamp behaviour; rate and force clamps.
  • Device staleness detection in the monitors.

Not yet confirmed

  • Reflection feel under real contact — force magnitudes are commanded, not measured.
  • Vibration root cause. Conservative gains shipped; whether it is contact-only or also free-motion is unresolved.
  • Driver capability states. Instrumented but not yet read, as the running nodes predate the rebuild.
  • Whether HD_MOTOR_TEMPERATURE is sensed or modelled.
